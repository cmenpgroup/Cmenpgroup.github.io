Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD)

Quantum Chromodynamics is the fundamental theory in particle physics that describes the strong interaction — one of the four known fundamental forces of nature. It governs the behavior of quarks and gluons, the building blocks of protons, neutrons, and other hadrons.

Key Features of QCD

  • Asymptotic Freedom: At very short distances (high energies), the strong force between quarks becomes weaker, allowing quarks to behave almost as free particles.
  • Confinement: At larger distances, the force between quarks grows stronger, preventing them from existing in isolation. This is why we only observe color-neutral hadrons.
  • Color Charge: Quarks carry one of three color charges (red, green, or blue), while gluons carry both a color and an anti-color.
  • Non-Abelian Gauge Theory: Based on the SU(3) symmetry group, making the theory mathematically complex and rich.

Medium Energy Nuclear Physics Context

Our group focuses on the application of QCD in the medium-energy regime, where the transition between perturbative and non-perturbative QCD occurs. Research includes studies of nucleon structure functions, generalized parton distributions (GPDs), and the dynamics of quarks and gluons inside nuclei.

We perform precision experiments and theoretical calculations to better understand phenomena such as:

  • Deep Inelastic Scattering (DIS).
  • Hadronization of the omega meson and the proton.
  • Medium modifications in nuclei.
  • Direct diquark scattering.

Our Mission

The Canisius Medium Energy Nuclear Physics Group is dedicated to advancing our understanding of the strong force through innovative experiments and theoretical developments. We collaborate with leading facilities worldwide, including Jefferson Laboratory, to explore the fundamental structure of matter.
