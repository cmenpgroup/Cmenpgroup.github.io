Jefferson Lab

Jefferson Lab, officially known as the Thomas Jefferson National Accelerator Facility, is a world-class U.S. Department of Energy national laboratory located in Newport News, Virginia.50 Scientists from around the globe come here to explore the fundamental building blocks of matter using the lab’s powerful Continuous Electron Beam Accelerator Facility (CEBAF). By firing precise beams of electrons at atomic nuclei, researchers probe the quarks and gluons that make up protons and neutrons, helping us understand the strong nuclear force and the very nature of the visible universe.

Beyond pure research, Jefferson Lab develops cutting-edge accelerator and computing technologies, partners with industry, and runs inspiring educational programs to train the next generation of scientists and engineers. In simple terms, it’s a place where curiosity about the tiniest pieces of our world drives big discoveries.

Hall B

Hall B at Jefferson Lab is one of the facility’s four experimental halls and home to the powerful CLAS12 detector, a large-acceptance spectrometer designed to capture a wide range of particles produced in high-energy electron collisions. Using the upgraded 12 GeV electron beam from CEBAF, researchers in Hall B study the internal structure of protons, neutrons, and atomic nuclei—probing how quarks and gluons give rise to the mass and properties of hadrons.

The hall supports large international collaborations that explore topics like nucleon structure, hadron spectroscopy, and the strong nuclear force, generating groundbreaking insights into the building blocks of matter. In everyday terms, Hall B is a high-tech “microscope” where scientists take ultra-detailed snapshots of the subatomic world to answer fundamental questions about our universe.
